# Exoplanet Basics

## What is an exoplanet?

An exoplanet is a planet outside our Solar System. Exoplanets orbit stars other than the Sun.

Our Solar System contains eight planets. A planet orbiting another star is called an exoplanet.

## Why are exoplanets difficult to see?

Stars are extremely bright compared with their planets, and distant planets are very small on astronomical scales. For this reason, astronomers often detect exoplanets indirectly by measuring their effects on their host stars.

Two important examples are:

- Transit: a planet passes in front of its star and causes a tiny decrease in observed brightness.
- Radial velocity: a planet's gravity causes its star to move slightly back and forth.

## Host star

The star that an exoplanet orbits is called its host star.

## Important idea

An artist's illustration of an exoplanet is not necessarily a photograph of its surface. For most distant exoplanets, scientists cannot directly resolve surface details.
