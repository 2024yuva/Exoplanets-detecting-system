# Exoplanet Types

Exoplanets come in a wide variety of sizes and compositions.

## Terrestrial / Rocky

Rocky planets are relatively small worlds with solid surfaces. Earth, Venus, Mars and Mercury are examples in our Solar System.

## Super-Earth

A super-Earth is generally a planet larger or more massive than Earth but smaller than the Solar System's ice giants. The term describes a size/mass category.

**Super-Earth does not mean Earth-like.**

A super-Earth can have very different atmosphere, temperature, composition and surface conditions from Earth.

## Neptunian

Neptunian planets are broadly similar in size category to Neptune and can have substantial gaseous envelopes.

## Gas giant

Gas giants are very large planets dominated by thick gaseous envelopes. Jupiter and Saturn are Solar System examples.

## Hot Jupiter

A hot Jupiter is a gas giant orbiting extremely close to its host star, resulting in a very short orbital period and very high temperatures.

## Mini-Neptune

A mini-Neptune is smaller than Neptune but larger than Earth, generally with a substantial atmosphere.

## Important caution

Planet categories are classification tools. They do not by themselves tell us whether a world is habitable or contains life.
